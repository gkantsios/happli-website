More of your future clients are asking a chatbot before they ask a friend. "Where should I get a lash lift in Tampa?" "Good brow studio near me open Saturday?" ChatGPT has [1.2 billion weekly users](https://openai.com/index/introducing-businesses-chatgpt/), and on November 10, 2026, OpenAI [opened Business Hub](https://openai.com/index/introducing-businesses-chatgpt/), a place for businesses to claim their profile in ChatGPT.

So how does a local lash, brow, nail, hair, facial, spray tan or med spa studio end up in the answer? Here's what's documented, and a list you can work through this week.

## How ChatGPT picks studios for "near me" questions

ChatGPT doesn't look up a hidden ranking. For questions like these it searches the web. OpenAI says it [rewrites the client's question into shorter search queries](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-searching-the-web-with-chatgpt) and may send them to search partners, with Microsoft named as one of them.

The local part matters most for you. OpenAI says ChatGPT [uses an approximate location from the person's IP address](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-searching-the-web-with-chatgpt), and gives an example: "good restaurants near me" from someone in San Francisco can become the search "top restaurants San Francisco." It may also share that general location with [providers of nearby business listings](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-searching-the-web-with-chatgpt).

So "lash lift near me" turns into something like "best lash lift [your city]." If your city and your services aren't written plainly on your pages and listings, that search has nothing to match.

FirstIndex, an SEO firm, logged [102 ChatGPT answers to 21 buyer questions](https://firstindex.com/guides/how-to-rank-in-chatgpt) on November 10, 2026. ChatGPT searched the web in every answer. About half its searches added the year, and about a third used words like "best," "top" or "vs." It searched a brand by name first in only 16 of the 102 answers. Their questions were about software agencies, not beauty, so treat it as a pattern, not a rule. The takeaway still fits a studio: if ChatGPT doesn't already know your name, it finds you through pages that answer the client's question.

## Step 1: Let ChatGPT's search crawler in

ChatGPT search reads the web with a crawler called OAI-SearchBot. OpenAI says sites that block it [won't be shown in ChatGPT search answers](https://platform.openai.com/docs/bots). Check two places:

- **Your robots.txt file.** Make sure it doesn't block OAI-SearchBot. OpenAI says a change takes [about 24 hours](https://platform.openai.com/docs/bots) to count.
- **Your bot protection.** If your site sits behind Cloudflare or another security service, a setting there can turn crawlers away even when robots.txt allows them. Cloudflare has [separate settings for search, agent and training crawlers](https://developers.cloudflare.com/bots/additional-configurations/block-ai-bots/) and lists OAI-SearchBot as an ["AI Search" crawler](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-crawl-control/reference/bots/). Leave the Search setting on Allow.

You don't have to give up your content to be found. OpenAI treats [GPTBot (model training) as a separate setting](https://platform.openai.com/docs/bots) from OAI-SearchBot (search). You can block one and allow the other.

## Step 2: Get your studio onto Bing, both the map and the index

Because ChatGPT sometimes sends searches to Microsoft, being findable on Bing helps.

- **Claim your Bing Places listing.** It's [free and puts you on Bing search and Bing Maps](https://blogs.bing.com/search/2025/10/Introducing-the-New-Bing-Places-for-Business-Built-for-Business-Owners,-Powered-by-Research/). If your Google Business Profile is already set up, Bing can [import it](https://blogs.bing.com/search/2025/10/Introducing-the-New-Bing-Places-for-Business-Built-for-Business-Owners,-Powered-by-Research/) so you don't retype everything. Start at [bing.com/forbusiness](https://www.bing.com/forbusiness).
- **Add your website to Bing Webmaster Tools** so Bing can crawl it.
- **Use IndexNow if your site supports it.** IndexNow tells Bing and other participating search engines that a page changed. Many site builders and CDNs [have it built in](https://www.bing.com/indexnow/getstarted). It doesn't guarantee a page gets indexed, [Bing says](https://www.bing.com/indexnow/getstarted), but it gets your new prices noticed sooner.

Keep your name, address, phone and hours the same on Google, Bing, Yelp, Instagram and your site, so nobody has to guess which is right.

## Step 3: Say what you do, where, and what it costs

When a client asks for "a gel manicure near downtown under $50," the answer has to come from something written down. Put these in plain words on your homepage and service pages:

- **Your services and your city in the first lines.** "Lash extensions, lifts and brow lamination in South Tampa" beats "Where beauty meets you."
- **Real prices,** or "from" prices, for your main services.
- **Hours,** including evenings and weekends.
- **Who you serve,** like first-timers, brides or sensitive skin, if that's your specialty.
- **Your policies:** deposits, cancellations and how far ahead to book.

FirstIndex found that ChatGPT's most-cited link for a company it named was usually [that company's homepage](https://firstindex.com/guides/how-to-rank-in-chatgpt). A tagline that never says "lash studio" or your city gives it nothing to quote.

## Step 4: Make your booking page public, specific and current

A recommendation only turns into a client if the next click lets them book. OpenAI's help docs say business agents in ChatGPT [can't make bookings](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001635-setting-up-your-business-on-chatgpt). They link to your website, and the client books there.

That makes your booking page the end of the trip. A good one:

- Is a normal public web page, not a PDF, an Instagram DM or a login screen.
- Lists your real services with names a client would search ("Classic full set," not "Package B").
- Shows real prices, durations and open times.
- Takes the deposit right there, so a stranger from a chatbot can commit in one visit.

## Step 5: Get onto local "best of" lists, honestly

In FirstIndex's test, [77% of answers cited at least one list or comparison page](https://firstindex.com/guides/how-to-rank-in-chatgpt), and most of those lists belonged to someone else. In their software category, directories barely showed up. Local beauty searches may behave differently, and we haven't tested them. But the method carries over:

1. Ask ChatGPT what your clients ask, like "best brow studio in [your city]," and open the sources it cites.
2. Note the local blogs, city magazines and "best of [city]" roundups that keep coming up.
3. Reach out where you're a real fit. Send the writer what they need: your services, neighborhood, price range and a booking link.

Never pay for fake reviews or buy your way onto a list. In the U.S., the FTC rule on reviews [bans buying or selling fake reviews](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/08/22/2024-18519/trade-regulation-rule-on-the-use-of-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials). Real reviews from real clients, asked for after a great visit, are the kind that last.

## Step 6: Claim your studio in ChatGPT Business Hub

Business Hub [launched in beta on November 10, 2026](https://openai.com/index/introducing-businesses-chatgpt/). Once you're verified, you can update your business details and see how often ChatGPT mentions you.

What OpenAI's help page says right now:

- **Local businesses can claim only an existing U.S. listing** for now. Eligibility [depends on your location and type of business](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001635-setting-up-your-business-on-chatgpt).
- **You verify** with your business phone, business email, a DNS record on your website or Shopify. Then you complete an [ID check through Persona](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001635-setting-up-your-business-on-chatgpt) before you can publish changes or see insights.
- **Claiming doesn't buy placement.** OpenAI says claiming [doesn't guarantee more visibility](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001635-setting-up-your-business-on-chatgpt), and ads don't change ChatGPT's own answers.

It's brand new and will change. Claim yours if you can, keep it accurate, and don't expect a ranking from it.

## Step 7: Keep your menu and dates fresh

ChatGPT adds the year to a lot of its searches. In FirstIndex's test, [54% of searches included "2026"](https://firstindex.com/guides/how-to-rank-in-chatgpt). An old price list with a 2023 promo on it looks stale to a person and to a search. When you change a service or price, update it everywhere and show an "updated" date on the page. OpenAI also notes ChatGPT [may draw on other sources](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001635-setting-up-your-business-on-chatgpt) besides your profile, so an outdated listing somewhere else can still show up.

## What you can skip

- **An llms.txt file.** SE Ranking looked at about 300,000 domains and found [no link between having one and getting cited](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/llms-txt-shows-no-clear-effect-on-ai-citations-based-on-300k-domains/561542/). It won't hurt, but don't spend your Saturday on it.
- **Paid "get ranked in ChatGPT" packages.** OpenAI says [advertising doesn't influence its independent answers](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001635-setting-up-your-business-on-chatgpt) and claiming doesn't guarantee placement. Plain, accurate pages do the work.

## How to tell if it's working

One test tells you very little. The same question can name you today and skip you tomorrow. Instead:

- Write down 5 to 10 questions your clients really ask, with your city in them.
- Ask each one a few times a month, in a fresh chat, and count how often your studio is named.
- If you claim Business Hub, check its insights. For a local business, OpenAI counts a mention [when ChatGPT shows a result for your location](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001635-setting-up-your-business-on-chatgpt), even if nobody clicks.
- Note in each new client's profile how they heard about you.

## Your checklist

- [ ] robots.txt allows OAI-SearchBot
- [ ] Cloudflare or other bot protection allows search crawlers
- [ ] Bing Places listing claimed, and site added to Bing Webmaster Tools
- [ ] Name, address, phone and hours match on Google, Bing, Yelp and your site
- [ ] Homepage says your services and city in the first lines
- [ ] Prices, hours and policies written out on your site
- [ ] Public booking page with real services, prices and open times, plus deposits
- [ ] Asked to join 2 or 3 local "best of" lists where you fit
- [ ] Business Hub claimed, if your studio is eligible
- [ ] Monthly check: same questions, count how often you're named

## FAQ

**Can I pay to be recommended by ChatGPT?**
No. OpenAI says ads [don't influence ChatGPT's independent answers](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001635-setting-up-your-business-on-chatgpt), and sponsored placements are [labeled and kept separate](https://openai.com/index/introducing-businesses-chatgpt/).

**Do I need to claim Business Hub to show up?**
No. ChatGPT searches the web either way. Claiming lets you manage your details and see insights, but OpenAI says it [doesn't guarantee more visibility](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001635-setting-up-your-business-on-chatgpt).

**Can a client book with me inside ChatGPT?**
Not today. OpenAI says business agents [can't make bookings](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001635-setting-up-your-business-on-chatgpt). They send people to your website, so your booking page has to be ready.

**Will blocking AI training hurt my chances?**
Not by itself. OpenAI treats [training (GPTBot) and search (OAI-SearchBot) as separate settings](https://platform.openai.com/docs/bots). Allow search, and choose what you like for training.

**How long do changes take?**
A robots.txt change takes [about 24 hours](https://platform.openai.com/docs/bots) for OpenAI. Business Hub edits are [reviewed before they appear](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001635-setting-up-your-business-on-chatgpt). New pages depend on when search engines crawl them, which is why IndexNow helps.
